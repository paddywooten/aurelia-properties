Home/About

A boutique brokerage, on purpose.

We were founded on a simple constraint: an agent can represent twelve to eighteen homes a year properly, and no more than that. Everything else about Aurelia follows from it.

Our story

Started in 2009 with one listing.

Elena Marchetti left a global brokerage after eleven years and took a single house on Sea Cliff — a mid-century property that had been on the market for a year and needed something more than a listing sign.

We rebuilt Aurelia around what that listing taught us. Buyers did not want more options; they wanted fewer, better ones, and someone willing to tell them the truth about each. Sellers did not want a photographer; they wanted a marketing plan and an honest weekly report.

Sixteen years later the practice is six partners and a team of twenty-two, operating across San Francisco, the Peninsula and the North Bay. We still take fewer listings than we are offered, and we still turn down work we do not think we can execute well.

We would rather lose a listing than take it badly. Elena Marchetti, Founding Partner

Principles

How we decide what to do

Fewer listings, deeper work

An agent carrying twenty active listings gives each about four hours a week. We cap the number at twelve to eighteen so that the number is twelve to eighteen.

One side of the table

Our buyer's agents are retained by the buyer and paid by the buyer. There is no split commission to negotiate, and no incentive to shade a recommendation.

Reporting you can act on

Every listing receives a written weekly report — showings, feedback, comparable activity and our recommendation for the week ahead.

Preparation over promotion

Marketing cannot rescue a poorly prepared property. We spend the first nine days fixing what buyers discount.

Advice you can act on

We will tell you when a price is wrong, when a market is not there yet, and when to walk away from a house you love.

Discretion as standard

Private sales, off-market access and NDAs are routine parts of our practice, not special arrangements.

The team

Who you will actually be working with

No juniors, no hand-offs. The agent you meet is the agent who does the work.

Offices
